# Ultima IV on the cart: what is different

Ultima IV: Quest of the Avatar is (c) 1985 Origin Systems, Inc. / Lord British. On the
cart it is the original C64 game, built from your own four disk images (Program,
Towne, Britannia, Underworld), with its disk access replaced by flash reads. Many of
the changes below come from **Ultima IV Remastered** by Per Olofsson (MagerValp). Its
code is (c) 2006-2015 Per Olofsson, Apache 2.0, and its new graphics are (c) 2015 Vanja
Utne. Each one taken from it is marked *(Remastered)*.

## No disks

- The game never asks for a disk. The "PLEASE PLACE THE ... DISK" prompts are gone.
- The main menu has no "Number of drives" line. "Return to the view" takes its row.
- The copy-protection check on the Program disk is switched off.

## Loading

- Every load comes from the cart at the tapecart's 2-bit speed, with the screen on. The
  original read each 256-byte block through the stock KERNAL at about 400 bytes a second.
  Measured in VICE, where timings are not cycle exact: a block takes about 38 ms
  instead of about 640 ms. Game main, the 80 blocks Journey Onward loads, takes about
  3.7 s instead of about 51 s.
- **The music keeps playing while the game loads.** In the original it froze during a
  load. Descend, Klimb, Mix, Quit & save, Search and Use also stopped the tune and
  started it again from the beginning. On the cart it plays on *(Remastered: the six
  stops removed; the loading with interrupts on is the cart's own)*. Character creation
  runs its own raster interrupt, so its loads still pause the music.
- The cart's access light is lit only while data moves.
- When you launch the game, the boot program's credits screen stays up for about four
  seconds, as it did while the original loaded. Any key skips it. Journey Onward skips
  it too.

## Journey Onward from the launcher

**J** on Ultima IV's page goes straight to your saved game. It skips the logo sequence
(about 20 seconds of waiting) and the title. The screen stays black for those seconds,
then the game appears.

## Saving

- Quit & save works as in the original. The game writes five 256-byte blocks of the
  Britannia disk, and each goes to its own place on the cart.
- **A power cut during a save loses nothing.** Each block is written to a spare place
  first, then over the old one. If the power goes during a save, you get either the old
  save or the new one, whole. Anything left half done is finished the next time the cart
  is switched on.
- **Quit & save works in a dungeon too**, and loading that game puts you back in the
  dungeon *(Remastered)*. In towns Quit & save still says "NOT HERE!".
- The original saved Britannia's temporary map (the surface's monsters and objects while
  you are in a town or dungeon) at every entry into a town, castle, keep or dungeon.
  The cart keeps it in RAM and stores it with your next Quit & save. A game you do not
  save loses it with everything else, as before.

## The options on Ultima IV's page

All are off on a new cart. Each key turns one on or off, and the cart remembers it.

| Key | Option (off is the original) | What "on" does |
|---|---|---|
| F | Food used while walking | Walking costs no food. Camping, and monsters that eat food, still cost food. |
| P | Turn passes when idle | No turn passes while you think. |
| U | Torches used up when lit | Igniting a torch keeps it *(Remastered's cheat)*. |
| M | Magic and items used up | Spells need no mixture and no MP. Jimmy uses no key. Peer uses no gem *(Remastered's cheats)*. |
| A | Attacks can't be avoided | "AVOID?" is asked when a monster attacks, at the bridge trolls and in a dungeon encounter. Y skips the fight *(Remastered's cheat)*. |
| W | Balloon drifts with wind | You steer the balloon with the direction keys while it flies, and the wind does not move it *(Remastered's cheat)*. |
| X | Teleport, transport, win | In Britannia, **T** teleports: to a (T)owne, (D)ungeon, (S)hrine, (L)ocation 1-8, or (C)oordinates given as four letters A-P, the way the sextant shows them. **B** on an empty square brings a H(orse), S(hip, which needs deep water beside you) or B(alloon). In combat, **X** wins the fight. With the option off, T is Talk, and B and X say "WHAT?" *(T and B: Remastered's in-game keys; X: after the trainer of Ultima III Gold, "cracked, trained and enhanced by MagerValp")*. |
| G | Graphics: original or Remastered | Remastered's tile set, font and character creation (below). |

### G: Remastered graphics

With G on, the game loads a second copy of the Program disk's graphics:

- **The tile set** *(Remastered, graphics by Vanja Utne)*. 184 of 256 tiles are
  redrawn, and each 8x8 cell of a tile has its own colours, in the style of Ultima V.
  This covers the overworld, the towns, combat and the title's view. The kit's renderer
  draws them. The font's frame and cursor glyphs and their colours change with the
  tiles.
- **Character creation** *(Remastered)*. It has Vanja Utne's seven pictures, the virtue
  cards with a third colour, the moongate rising as sprites, and a fade between
  pictures.

With G off, everything is drawn in the original graphics. Your saved game is the same
either way.

## Always on: from Ultima IV Remastered

These are part of the cart's Ultima IV with no option. All of them come from Remastered,
except where a line says otherwise.

**Bug fixes**

- The stack stays balanced when a monster attacks you, when you meet the bridge trolls
  and when the party dies. In the original each of these leaves two bytes behind, and
  enough of them over a long session can crash the game to `READY.`.
- Character creation does not hang at random (the original can).
- Meditating at a shrine: answering 0 cycles does not count as 256 (in the original it
  does). 1, 2 and 3 cycles give the virtue's first, second and third hint, where the
  original always gives the first.
- Destard's and Hythloth's rooms that put the party inside a wall have proper start
  positions. Hythloth's room 9 gets the trigger that opens its west wall.
- After Lord British resurrects the party, his castle's music plays.
- DEL works when you enter a number (giving gold) and when you Use stones in a dungeon.
- B on a dungeon ladder does not board a frigate.
- Enter is refused while the balloon is flying. Unlike Remastered, the cart lets you
  enter with a landed balloon.
- A game saved while flying the balloon loads still flying.
- Attacking something that is not a monster gives a proper foe. A sick person fights
  as a beggar, the water in Castle Britannia as deep water, and a lord or lady as a
  knight. A chest cannot be attacked, and the ankh and camp fires keep still in combat.
- After a wrong answer at the Codex, being thrown out of the Abyss leaves the stack
  intact. The main menu's and character creation's starts do too.
- J at the title with no saved game does not hang after "NO ACTIVE GAME!": after two
  seconds the title comes back (the cart's own fix; Remastered's version still hangs).
- Typos: "WH0-", "-GOLD!", "THE ALTER ROOM" and, in the Abyss, "THE ALTER" and "THYNE".

**Conversations**

- Two conversations that no one in the original leads to are reachable: Thevel in
  Britain has his own conversation rather than a copy of Joe's, and the gate guards of
  Serpent's Hold have theirs.
- Questions the original never asks come up (Water in Castle Britannia, Shamino,
  Charm, Alkerion, Thevel, Michelle). Keywords that could not be typed are corrected
  (Estro's, a beggar's in Yew). Some spellings are corrected too.
- **Dialogue in mixed case**: every conversation, Lord British, his help, the talk
  overlay's own lines and the shopkeepers use Remastered's capitals and small letters.
  Ten conversations too long for the original format keep the original wording in
  places. Lord British's words at a resurrection are recased by the cart, since
  Remastered left them in capitals. The game's own messages stay in capitals.

**Graphics and play**

- **The dungeon view** is Remastered's: faster, monsters and items coloured, the view
  darkening as a torch burns down, and slightly different proportions.
- **Peering at a gem** shows a colour map: water blue and grass green on the surface,
  and coloured features in the Underworld. While the map is up, the side panel's text
  looks garbled, as in Remastered.
- **"EXIT?" at a town's edge.** Only Y leaves. Any other key cancels the step.
- **Typed-ahead keys are kept.** The original took one key and threw away the rest.
  **Joystick 2** moves the party, and fire is Enter in Britannia, Talk in a town and
  Attack in combat.
- **The active character in combat.** Press 1-8 to play one character, 0 for the whole
  party.

Not taken from Remastered: mixed case for the game's own messages, and lower-case name
entry. Ultima V's "Transfer from Ultima IV" expects the name in capitals.

## Always on: from Sean Gugler's continuation of Remastered

Sean Gugler continued Ultima IV Remastered (versions 2.3.0 to 2.4.1) and found more bugs
in the original. The cart fixes these, mostly his way:

**Crashes**

- Every moongate jump left two bytes on the stack, so after about a hundred jumps in one
  session the game could crash to `READY.`. Ztats in combat, Z-down on the lowest level
  and "ALL MUST USE THE SAME EXIT!" upset the stack too.
- The party dying when the ship sinks (in a whirlpool, a twister or under fire) left
  more on the stack than Remastered's fix took off. Now the stack is set back to where
  every command starts.

**Bugs**

- After a night at the inn, a member who was asleep wakes up Good. In the original they
  got the status 'A': not alive, not dead, out of every fight, until Lord British.
- Fighting townsfolk, guards, children and the like: their strength comes from the right
  entry of the game's table. A beggar was as tough as a lich, a child as a cyclops.
- Ztats shows the 3-part key when you have the bell or the book but not the candle.
- The healer in Serpent's Hold answers when you talk to him.
- Destroying the skull at the Abyss raises every virtue, as it was meant to.
- Giving your last gold piece to a beggar raises Sacrifice, as the shrine of Sacrifice
  advises. The original raised Honor.
- A healer's spell flashes the view, as other spells do.
- Hawkwind's virtue reward and meditating at a shrine keep one timer between them. The
  original checked it against a different turn counter for each, so each could upset
  the other's wait.
- Finding the bell or another item while searching no longer delays the next mandrake or
  nightshade.
- Keys pressed while meditating are dropped before the shrine's next question.
- Using the skull in Britannia makes its sound.
- Katrina gives one reason not to join, not "humbleexperienced".
- Browning, Aristotle, Santayana and Slysam ask questions that test humility, worded like
  the others that do, and now count as those do. A boastful yes costs Humility.
- The bridge trolls do not attack while you fly over the bridge in the balloon.
- Attacking a townsperson shows the lost virtue in the ankh at once.
- A chest dropped by a townsperson cannot be talked to.
- An east-facing horse does not flicker in combat.
- Getting off a horse in a crowded town (Lycaeum, Empath Abbey, Serpent's Hold and
  others) no longer makes someone vanish. The horse can be boarded again wherever it was
  put.
- Dungeon Wrong: two ladders that led nowhere connect.
- With the torch fully lit, the dungeon view is white, as in the original. It turns grey
  as the torch burns down, as in Remastered.

The cart's own changes in the same vein:

- Dungeon Covetous: the ladder down on level 6 that ended on level 7's plain floor is
  gone. The ladder up on that square stays.
- With the X option, **T**eleport takes away Britannia's monsters only. Your ship,
  horse, balloon and any chests stay where they were.

## Faster steps in towns

A step in a town or castle takes about 20% less time. The text window's scroll, the
town view's buffer and its line of sight are rewritten (the cart's own). Each does
exactly what the original did. Measured in VICE in Britain: 0.327 s a step becomes
0.264 s with the original graphics, and 0.307 s becomes 0.245 s with Remastered's. A
blocked step is slower on purpose: its "BLOCKED!" sound is generated by the CPU, and
the game waits for it to finish.

## Covetous, room 11

Two versions of the Underworld disk exist. In Covetous's room 11 (level 7), the Ultima
Trilogy's disk has floor at one square of the middle corridor. The 1986 release has a
wall there, which cuts off the exit that the room's trigger opens. The cart always uses
the floor. If your image of the Underworld disk has the wall, the build accepts it and
puts the floor back.

## Export, import and other games

- **E** and **I** on the page copy the saved game to and from a real disk. **(D)isks**
  writes the five save blocks to a copy of the Britannia disk, in drive 8-11. The
  Trilogy's and the 1986 Britannia disks are both accepted, and the game's disk number
  is checked too. **(F)ile** writes `ULTIMA IV SAVE` on any disk (6 blocks).
- A game saved in a dungeon is exported as it is, and the page says so. The original
  game cannot resume in a dungeon: it would put the party on the surface at the
  dungeon's coordinates.
- Ultima V's "Transfer from Ultima IV" reads your Ultima IV save from the cart. Ultima
  VI's page can transfer your Avatar from it.
- The cart runs on real hardware.

## Known limitations

- A dungeon save exported to disk starts on the surface in the original game (above).
- The music pauses during loads in character creation.
- Peering at a gem in colour garbles the side panel's text while the map is shown.
