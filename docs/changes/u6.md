# Ultima VI on the cart: what is different

Ultima VI: The False Prophet (C64 version, (c) Origin Systems / Lord British) runs from the
cartridge with its six disks -- Surface (1A), Dungeon (1B), Populace A, B and C (2A, 2B,
3A) and Game (3B) -- in flash. This page lists what a player will notice. How it is done is
in `docs/developer/u6.md`; what is known about the original game is in
`docs/reversing-notes/u6.md`.

(Ultima VI also exists on its own as a 1 MiB EasyFlash build with the same game changes; see
`docs/easyflash.md`.)

## The disks you build it from

The build needs the six original disks. **The Surface and Dungeon disks must be unplayed
copies.** Ultima VI keeps its whole world -- every object, every opened door, every item
moved -- on those two disks and never resets it; the manual tells players to make copies
and play only on the copies. A Surface or Dungeon disk that has been played on is refused
by the build with a message saying so; nothing is repaired or patched up. The exception is
a Surface disk on which a character was made but never saved: its world is still unplayed,
and the build takes it, with the character removed. A played pair of
disks is not wasted, though: once the cart is built, the page's import (below) brings that
game in as a saved game.

## No disks, no swaps, shorter pauses

- No disk prompts and no disk swaps: the Surface disk for a save while in a dungeon, the
  Populace disks for conversations, all of it is in flash.
- The game's own fastloader was quick, so the gain is less in raw speed than in seeking,
  spin-up and swaps. A map-window shift (the game saves and loads its world every time the
  party nears the edge of the 256 x 256-tile area it holds in memory) takes about 46 s from
  a 1541 and about 8.5 s from the cart, measured in an emulator.
- Interrupts stay on while the cart is read, so the music during the opening keeps
  playing and the game keeps reading the keyboard.

## Saves

Ultima VI saves as it always did: Shift-S, and automatically at every map-window shift and
every level change.

- **Every save goes into a log** in the cart's flash: the new version is written next to
  the old one, never over it. If the power goes in the middle of a save, the game comes
  back with the save before it.
- **The log is tidied up when it runs short.** A save writes about 30 pages, and the log
  has room for a session's worth. When fewer than 128 pages are left, the next power-on
  tidies it before the game starts ("tidying the world of ultima vi" and a progress bar,
  white on black): about 12 s typically, up to about 36 s. If a save finds the log full
  during play, it tidies it there and then, with a spinning cursor in the cell after the
  command prompt `>`, and goes on with the save: 15 to 50 s. Nothing is ever dropped. A
  power cut during the tidying leaves the log as it was.
- **Character creation resets the world.** On the original, a new character on a used
  disk kept the old world. On the cart, creating a character starts from the pristine
  world, as fresh copies of the disks would.
- **Shift-L's "LOAD Game ?" works.** In the original the answer was taken the wrong way
  round: Y did nothing and N reloaded the last save, throwing away everything since. Now Y
  loads and N goes back to play.

## The access light

While the cart is busy during play, one cell of the screen frame turns white: row 24,
column 23, just below the command prompt's cursor. Ultima VI has the longest pauses of the
six games, and the cart's own LED may be out of sight. The cell is dark the rest of the
time. (The LED on the cart is lit only while data moves, in every game.)

## Faster on the map

The map view's routines are rewritten to do the same thing in fewer cycles: the line of
sight, the light map, the drawing of the 9 x 9 view, and the placing of objects when new
map chunks scroll in. The screen is the same; measured in an emulator:

- a normal step takes about 0.27 s instead of 0.31 s where something blocks the view
  (a castle, a town, a forest), and about 0.23 s instead of 0.31 s on open ground;
- the step every eighth square, where a new strip of the map is read, takes about 0.46 s
  instead of about 0.7 s;
- the pause after Get, Drop, Move, many Use actions, spells and combat, where the game
  places the window's objects again, takes about 0.17 s instead of about 0.87 s.

## With a RAM Expansion Unit

With a Commodore REU of 256 KB or more (a 1764, a 1750, or a compatible one; not the
128 KB 1700) plugged in, the game uses it on its own; there is no switch.

- **Reads**: every overlay, the tiles reloaded after a conversation and the rest of the
  game's sector reads come from the REU after their first use. A large overlay (Cast,
  Talk, combat) takes about 1.1 s from the cart and a few hundredths of a second from the
  REU.
- **Saves**: a save writes only what changed since the last save of the same session,
  found by the REU's own compare (no checksums). A window shift takes about 5 s instead of
  about 7.5 s, a save about 2.5 s instead of about 5 s (emulator measurements), and the log
  fills five to seven times more slowly. The first save after power-on writes everything.
- The page shows what was found, in grey below the saved game: `REU: 512 KB, for loads
  and saves`; `for loads only` with `its verify command failed` (the REU's compare does not
  work, so only the read cache is used); `REU: 128 KB, too small to use`; nothing without
  one. An Action Replay's RAM is not mistaken for an REU.

The REU never holds anything that matters: it is cleared at every launch, and the save
always goes to the cart.

## Input

- **A quick second tap of the same key is not lost.** The original counted a second
  tap of the same key as the first one still held, and dropped it unless it was held long
  enough to repeat: Return or space twice through a conversation, Y after Y. Now a tap is
  taken; held keys repeat as before, and a key that bounces for a moment counts once.
- **A tap of a direction key or the joystick is not lost.** The game looks at the
  direction keys and the joystick once a turn; a short tap between two looks went unseen.
  Now the first direction pressed since the last look is kept until the game reads it.
  Tapping ahead during a slow step moves one square more at most, and two taps never merge
  into a diagonal.
- **Return confirms when tapped.** Choosing a target or an item with Return only worked
  if Return was still down when the game looked; on the cart a tap confirms too.
- **Space goes on in the text boxes of the opening and introduction.** After the title
  menu, the original took only the joystick's button there.

(Ultima VI moves and aims with `+ - £ / @ ↑ / : ; =` and the joystick in port 2, not with
the cursor keys; Return confirms.)

## Fixes to the game

- **The shrines' level check.** The C64 version compared only the low byte of the
  experience with each shrine's threshold, so a level came too early or too late. The
  shrines now check the whole number, exactly as the PC version does.
- **Resting with a guard.** When the guard raised the alarm ("I hear something!"), the
  original went on resting: the party stayed asleep, the view and the campfire moved with
  the keys, and moves passed or failed at random. Now the alarm ends the rest and wakes the
  party. And the last member of the party can stand guard (the original refused them).
- **The ending plays.** It carried its own disk loader; on the cart it loads from flash.
- **The text box's top line is steady** in the opening, the introduction and the ending
  (it flickered now and then, mostly on PAL machines).
- **The copy-protection questions accept any answer** (Lord British's, Mariah's and
  Selganor's). "Bye" still ends the conversation.

The game's own cheat menu stays as it was: talking to yourself (T001). Its "Exp =" shows
experience divided by 50, a side effect of the shrine fix.

## The page

Ultima VI has no options on the page. What it offers:

- the saved game: the party's names and hit points;
- **J, Journey onward**: past the opening, straight into the saved game (under ten
  seconds);
- **T, the Avatar from Ultima IV or V**: offered while the Avatar is as character
  creation left it. The PC version of Ultima VI imports the Avatar at creation; the C64
  version never could. The page lists the Avatar of Ultima IV and of Ultima V from their
  saves on the cart and converts by the PC's rules: experience divided by 10, strength,
  dexterity and intelligence halved from Ultima IV and kept from Ultima V, all 1-30, never
  below what creation gave (so level 3, 4 or 5). Hit points are 30 a level, magic points
  twice the intelligence. Name and sex stay as created. The game then starts by Journey
  Onward with that Avatar.
- **E and I, export and import**, to the game's own Surface and Dungeon disks (copies:
  the whole world, all 84 object files and the save area, with a disk directory that is
  consistent afterwards, so the original game plays it) or to one file, `ULTIMA VI SAVE`,
  on any disk (only what differs from the pristine world, 3-15 KiB). An import checks
  everything before the cart changes. An export to real disks is slow: about 3.5 minutes to
  a 1541, swaps not counted.
- **R, reset**: back to the pristine world and no saved game.

## Known limitations

- The tidying of the log takes up to about a minute when it happens during play (see
  Saves).
- Without an REU every save writes all of its about 30 pages, unchanged or not.
- The REU's 64-byte map reads (every eighth step) are not cached.
