# Ultima V on the cart: what is different

Ultima V: Warriors of Destiny is (c) 1988 Origin Systems, Inc. / Lord British. On the
cart it is the original C64 game, built from your own eight disk images (Program 1A,
Dungeon 1B, Britannia 2A, Underworld 2B, Towne 3A, Dwelling 3B, Castle 4A, Keep 4B),
with its disk access replaced by flash reads. Nothing in the cart's game is taken from
**Ultima V Remastered** by Drunella (code Apache 2.0); see "Other projects" at the end.

## No disks

- The game never asks for a disk. Every "insert ... disk" prompt is gone, and so is the
  drive-type question at boot (1541/1571 or Enhancer 2000/MSD).
- The main menu has no "Number of drives" line; the cursor stops at the fifth entry.
  The menu's letter keys A-O still play the fifteen songs where there is music (a C128,
  or a C64 with an Action Replay or the Ultima VI EasyFlash, below).
- The original has no copy protection to switch off: the unformatted track 6 on the
  Program disk is never read.

## Loading

- On a plain C64 every load comes from the cart at the tapecart's 1-bit speed, with the
  screen on and no flicker. Per byte that is not faster than the original's own
  fastloader, but there are no seeks, no drive spin-up and no disk swaps: a town map or
  a dungeon level comes straight from flash.
- With an Action Replay or the Ultima VI EasyFlash plugged in (below), or in C128 mode,
  loads use the faster 2-bit transfer, still with the screen on. Measured in VICE (not
  cycle exact) on the same 160 loads: about 1.2 times faster with the Action Replay or the
  EasyFlash, 1.25-1.35 times in C128 mode. Most of the game's files are stored packed,
  and unpacking them takes as long as before.
- The cart's access light is lit only while data moves.

## Journey Onward from the launcher

**J** on Ultima V's page (offered when there is a saved game) starts the game as if J
had been held while it loaded: straight to your saved game, past the title and the
attract sequence.

A new game, too, is played through Journey Onward: after character creation the game
plays its title and returns to its menu, as the original does.

## Saving

- Quit & save works as in the original. It writes six files' worth of state: the roster,
  the party data and the three object lists for Britannia, and the Underworld's object
  list.
- **Switching off during a save loses nothing.** Each part is written twice, first to a
  spare block, then over the old one. If the power goes in the middle you get either the
  save before or the new one, whole; when it went during the second write, the cart
  finishes the job the next time it is switched on, before anything else starts.
- **Exception: in C128 mode** saves are written in place, without the spare block, so a
  power cut during a C128 save can lose that save.
- A new cart, or **R** (Reset) on the page, gives the state of an unplayed game: the
  game says "No active game!" until you create a character. (This empty state is built
  by the cart, not copied from your Britannia disk, whose save files carry leftovers
  from mastering that the game never reads.)

## The options on Ultima V's page

All are off on a new cart, so the game plays as it always did. Each key turns one on or
off, and the cart remembers it.

| Key | Option | With it off |
|---|---|---|
| F | Food used while walking | No ration is taken at 06, 12 and 18 o'clock while you travel, and nobody starves. Camping and monsters that eat food still cost food. |
| U | Torches used up when lit | Igniting a torch lights it but keeps the count; you still need one to light. |
| M | Magic and items used up | Cast needs no mixed spell and no magic points (the level check stays, so a spell above your level still fails); a key never breaks while jimmying a lock (you still need one); View uses no gem (you still need one). |
| X | F7 teleport, B vehicle | On: on the surface of Britannia or the Underworld, **F7** asks for `X-` and `Y-` and moves you there, in the world you are in: up to three digits each, 0-255, RETURN after one or two. A turn passes. Anything else, or a number above 255, gives up, and no turn passes. **B**(oard) on foot where there is nothing to board asks `H)orse C)arpet` / `S)hip sK)iff?`, and you are in the one you choose (any other key: nothing, and no turn passes); a ship comes as the shipwright sells it, its hull at 99 and two skiffs aboard. X(it) leaves it behind like any other. With the option off, F7 and B are "What?" as before |

Ultima V has no idle turn to switch off, and no G option.

The squares of both worlds, and where every towne, castle, keep, dungeon and shrine is,
are in the lists `make maps` writes from your disks (`build/maps/u5/britannia_places.txt`,
`underworld_places.txt`): Yew is at x 58, y 43, for instance, so F7 5 8 RETURN 4 3 puts you
outside it, and E enters. Where you stand: C= and 1 together (the game's own debug
display) prints five two-digit hexadecimal numbers, the last two X and Y. Neither a
teleport nor a vehicle looks at the square: on a ship you may end up on land, or on foot
in the water or in the mountains; teleport again, or X-it.
On a C64 the DEL key gives the game the same code as F7, so it teleports too.

## Ultima IV transfer, from the cart

"Transfer From Ultima IV" on the main menu reads your Ultima IV saved game from the same
cart, instead of asking for the Ultima IV Britannia disk. Its prompt says so: press a
key to transfer the party from your Ultima IV save, or (A) to abort. Play Ultima IV on
the cart and save there first.

## Music on a C64, with an Action Replay

The original plays its fifteen songs only on a C128, from that machine's second RAM
bank. On the cart, a C64 plays them too when an **Action Replay 4.2, 5 or 6** is plugged
in: start the AR's fastload mode, then load the tapecart as usual. The game finds the
AR's 8 KiB of RAM at boot, puts the music player there and loads each song from the cart
as it is needed. Without an AR nothing changes; another cartridge with RAM in the same
place (an EasyFlash) is not taken for one.

- The game asks for its songs where the C128 version does (the title, the overworld,
  towns, dungeons, combat, the ending), and "Volume" in the game turns the music off
  and on.
- It is muted for the moment of each load or save, as on a C128.
- Loads become faster too (above).
- Tested with the Action Replay 6 ROM. Other versions and clones have not been tried.

## Music on a C64, with the Ultima VI EasyFlash

The collection's Ultima VI EasyFlash cartridge (`make ef`, [easyflash.md](../easyflash.md))
can stand in for the Action Replay. Plug it into the expansion port and the tapecart into
the tape port, and press **5** on the EasyFlash's page: it starts the tapecart's
Ultima V with the music and the faster loads, all fifteen songs coming from the
EasyFlash. The EasyFlash's page has the 5 only when Ultima V's disks were there when it
was built.

- The game, its saves and its options are the tapecart's: the cheats set on Ultima V's
  page on the tapecart apply (the EasyFlash shows which while it starts the game; it has
  no screen to change them), and the game saves to the tapecart as always.
- The music plays as with the Action Replay (above), muted for the moment of each load
  or save as there; but the songs are never loaded from the tapecart, so a new song
  starts without waiting for one.
- Both carts have to be from the same build: the EasyFlash checks that the tapecart's
  Ultima V is the one it was built with, and says so if it is not.
- After a power cut during a save, start the tapecart on its own once (the EasyFlash
  says so): the tapecart finishes the save at power-on, which the EasyFlash cannot do
  for it.
- Nothing on the tapecart changes for this; without the EasyFlash, Ultima V plays as
  before.

## Ultima V on a Commodore 128

On a C128, Ultima V can run in C128 mode, as the original does when booted on one: music
from the second RAM bank, eleven command overlays and a map kept in RAM instead of
reloaded, and 2-bit loads.

1. On Ultima V's page, press **C** and give it a disk in the drive shown: it writes a
   small program, `ULTIMA V 128`. It is a plain program file, not a boot sector.
2. On the C128, with the cart in the tape port, from a fresh start (no GRAPHIC):
   `DLOAD"ULTIMA V 128"` (add `,U9` and so on for another drive) and `RUN`.

The disk keeps working after the cart is updated: the program takes everything else from
the cart. The saved game is the same one the C64 plays, so a game saved in C128 mode
goes on from Journey Onward on a C64, and the other way round. The options set on the
page apply in C128 mode too.

Limitations: saves in C128 mode are not power-cut safe (above), and C128 mode has been
run in an emulator only, not on a real C128.

## Faster steps

The view, the text window and the townspeople's path-finding are rewritten to do the
same work in fewer cycles. The screen after a walk is the same as in the original.
Measured in VICE (not cycle exact), with sound off:

| Where | Original | On the cart |
|---|---|---|
| Britannia, a step | about 0.30 s | about 0.21 s |
| A town, a step | about 0.52 s | about 0.40 s |
| C128 mode, Britannia | about 0.41 s | about 0.32 s |

Steps in a busy town, where many townspeople search for a way, still take longer.

## A rare crash after a reset

The original tells a C128 from a C64 by one byte of memory that is RAM on a C64. A
program that left a particular value there before a reset (the reset keeps RAM) sent the
game down its C128 path on a C64, where it crashed at once. The cart clears that byte
before starting any game.

## Export, import and other games

- **E** exports the saved game, **I** imports one, through drive 8-11:
  - **(D)isks**: copies of your Britannia (2A) and Underworld (2B) disks, written as the
    original game writes them, so the export plays in the original too;
  - **(F)ile**: one file, `ULTIMA V SAVE` (13 blocks), on any disk, read only by this
    collection.
  An import checks everything it reads before it changes the cart.
- Ultima VI's page can transfer your Avatar from this game (T on that page).
- The save editor for the PC reads and writes Ultima V's saves.

## Known limitations

- Saves made in C128 mode are written in place, not through the spare block.
- The Action Replay music has been tried with the AR 6 ROM only.
- On a plain C64 loads run at the tapecart's 1-bit speed (the faster read needs room in
  memory the game does not have).

## Other projects

**Ultima V Remastered** by Drunella (code (c) 2025 Drunella, Apache 2.0) is a separate
build of Ultima V for EasyFlash, 1581 and BackBit. The cart's game takes no code or data
from it. Two of this repository's PC-side tools draw on it as a reference: the save
editor's Ultima V pages take several roster fields (weapons, rings, amulets, potions,
karma) from its save editor's layout, and the map renderer follows the map layout its
tools read and uses its legend for the dungeon maps.
