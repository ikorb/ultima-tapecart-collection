# Ultima II on the cart: what is different

Ultima II: The Revenge of the Enchantress (c) Origin Systems / Lord British. The cart runs
the original C64 program with its disk access replaced by reads from the cartridge's flash.
Everything you see and play is the original game, apart from what is listed here.

## Which disks the build needs

Ultima II came on one double-sided disk: side A ("Program + Galactic") and side B
("Player Master"). The build reads both from your own images.

- **Side A must be the Origin edition, the one from the Ultima Trilogy.** Sierra's 1984
  side A is a different edition: it has no separate `INTRO` file (that part of the game is
  packed into its boot program, and is a different build of it). The build refuses it, and
  `make release-scan` names it as Sierra's.
- **Side B may come from either edition**: the two are identical.
- A **played** Player Master disk is fine. The cart starts with a fresh player disk, which
  the build rebuilds from templates the game itself keeps on that disk. A saved game found
  on your disk can be put on the cart afterwards (`make release-import`, or Import on the
  game's page from a copy of the disk).

## No disks, no prompts

- Both sides are in flash. There is no disk swapping, and the game never asks for a disk:
  the "INSERT PLAYER DISK", "INSERT PROGRAM DISK" and "WRONG DISK!!" prompts are gone.
- The copy-protection check (which needs a deliberately damaged sector on the original
  disk) passes without a drive.
- Files that exist under the same name on both sides with different contents (twelve of
  them: some maps, their monsters and their conversations) are told apart the way the game
  intends: Earth's come from the Player side, the planets' from the Galactic side.

## Loading

- Loads are many times faster than the original's, which used the stock KERNAL routines
  (about 400 bytes a second). The cart reads at about 8.5 KB/s with the screen on (2 bits
  at a time, timed around the screen's badlines; 1 bit at a time while a sprite is shown),
  and most files are stored compressed.
- The screen does not flicker or go blank during loads. "ONE MOMENT PLEASE!" still
  appears briefly, as it does in the original.
- Pressing RESTORE while the cart is transferring data does nothing, so it cannot crash
  a load.
- The cartridge's LED is lit while data moves.

## Saving

The game saves where it always did: the player record when you save, and a map's
inhabitants (the `MOB` and `MON` files) each time you leave a map on Earth.

- **Saves go into a write log** in the cartridge's flash: each save is added behind the
  previous ones, and the old version is never overwritten. When the log is full it copies
  the newest version of everything into its other half; that save takes a few seconds
  longer.
- **Switching off during a save loses nothing**: at the next start you get the game as it
  was just before that save or just after it, never a broken player.
- Updating the cart with a new build's update image keeps the saved game (see the
  player's guide on flashing).

## Faster steps

A move's footstep sounds as it did: two clicks on foot, three on a horse, at the
original's spacing. But the game no longer stands still while they play; in the original
that waiting was more than half of every step. The moved view now appears about 0.15 s
sooner. Measured in VICE (not cycle exact):

| | Original | On the cart |
|---|---|---|
| A step on foot, from the key to the next prompt | about 0.23 s | about 0.09 s |
| A step on a horse | slower still (a third click) | about 0.09 s |
| Holding a direction key | about 0.24 s a step | 0.20 s a step |

Two moves are at least 0.2 s apart, so a direction held down walks five steps a second,
a little faster than the original, and keeps the clicks apart. A key tapped after a
pause moves at once. The frigate, which never waited, is as it was.

## The game's page in the launcher

Picking Ultima II in the list opens its page:

- the saved game: the player's name, hit points and food;
- **L** launches, **J** (Journey onward) goes straight into the saved game, past the
  title (offered when there is a saved game);
- **E** exports and **I** imports the saved game, either to copies of your own Player
  Master disk (it asks for the "Player" disk; the BAM name of Sierra's player disk is
  accepted too) or as one file, `ULTIMA II SAVE`, on any disk. An exported Player disk
  plays in the original game. An import checks everything before it changes the cart.
- **R** resets the saved game to a fresh player disk (asks first).

### Options

All are off on a new cart, so the game plays as it always did. The cart remembers them.

| Key | Option | When turned off / on |
|---|---|---|
| F | Food used while walking | Off: moving, passing and dungeon turns cost no food. Other food losses stay. |
| P | Turn passes when idle | Off: no turn passes while you wait at the command prompt, in a dungeon, or at a direction prompt. |
| U | Torches used up when lit | Off: igniting a torch in a dungeon keeps it. |
| X | Locate, teleport, vehicle | On: on the surface of Earth (any era) or a planet, **F1** shows where you are (`POSITION X-30 Y-31`, as F7 takes it) and no turn passes; **F7** asks for `X-` and `Y-` (two digits each, 00-63) and moves you there; **B**(oard) where there is nothing to board offers **(H)orse, (F)rigate, (P)lane**. No rocket: launching stays the way between Earth and the planets. In towns, and with the option off, F1 and F7 are "ILLEGAL COMMAND!" and B says "WHAT?" as before. |
| V | Counts wrap past 99/9999 | On (the original): numbers wrap, so 99 weapons plus one is 00, and 9999 gold plus five is 0004. Off: gold, experience and food stop at 9999; attributes, weapons, armour, spells, items, torches, keys, tools, helms and tri-lithium stop at 99. |

The X option follows the trainer keys of "Ultima III Gold" (cracked, trained and enhanced
by MagerValp, released at BFP 2007) and of "Ultima IV Remastered" by Per Olofsson
(MagerValp); the code for Ultima II is this collection's own.

About V: every number in the player record is a two-digit (or four-digit) decimal, and the
original adds to it without checking for overflow. Hit points are the exception on the
C64: the one thing that raises them gives nothing once they are at 9900 or above, so they
never wrap.

## Changes in behaviour

- **"THIS DISK HAS A PLAYER NAMED ..."**: on the original, any key other than the one to
  continue went back to "insert player disk", so you could put in another disk. With no
  disks, the cart goes back to the title screen instead, where you can play the existing
  player or create a new one. (Without this, the game would find the same player at once
  and ask again, endlessly.)
- **Shift+C= does nothing** during the game. In the original it flips the character set
  for a moment, until the game's display puts it back.

## Known limitations

- The cart holds one player, like one Player disk. To keep others, export them to disk
  or to a file and import the one you want to play.
